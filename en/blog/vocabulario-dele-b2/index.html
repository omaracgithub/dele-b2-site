Key Vocabulary for DELE B2 — Essential List with Examples

Updated in June 2026 · 12 min read

The B2 level of DELE requires a much broader lexical mastery than B1. Everyday vocabulary is no longer enough: you need to handle current terms, debate ideas, and abstract topics with ease. Here is a selection of essential vocabulary organized by thematic fields, with usage examples and strategies for effective memorization.

What level of vocabulary does DELE B2 require?

According to the Common European Framework of Reference, a B2 speaker must be able to understand the main ideas of complex texts on both concrete and abstract topics, including technical discussions in their field of specialization. This translates to an active vocabulary of approximately 4,000-5,000 words, compared to 2,500-3,000 for B1.

In the DELE B2 exam, vocabulary appears in all tests:

  • Reading comprehension: newspaper articles, essays, and informative academic texts
  • Listening comprehension: news, lectures, and debates
  • Written expression: formal letters, opinion articles, and reviews
  • Oral expression: presentations and debates on current topics

Politics and Society

Political and social topics are recurring in DELE B2. Mastering this vocabulary will allow you to understand news and participate in debates.

  • Citizenship — Active citizenship involves participating in the public affairs of your community.
  • Inequality — Economic inequality remains one of the main challenges of modern societies.
  • Legislation — The new data protection legislation will come into effect next month.
  • Demonstration — Thousands of people attended the demonstration to demand better working conditions.
  • Corruption — Cases of corruption have eroded citizens' trust in institutions.
  • Democracy — Participatory democracy goes beyond the simple act of voting every four years.
  • Immigration — Immigration policies must balance humanitarian reception with social integration.
  • Fundamental rights — Fundamental rights are enshrined in the Constitution and are inalienable.
  • Governance — Good governance requires transparency and accountability.
  • Ideology — Their political ideology is situated in the center of the spectrum, without extremism.

Environment

The environment is one of the star topics of DELE B2. It frequently appears in reading comprehension tests and oral expression topics.

  • Sustainability — Sustainability is not just a trend, but an urgent necessity for future generations.
  • Carbon footprint — Reducing our individual carbon footprint is important, but corporate policies are decisive.
  • Biodiversity — The loss of biodiversity threatens the balance of ecosystems globally.
  • Renewable energies — Investment in renewable energies has tripled in the last decade.
  • Pollution — Air pollution causes thousands of premature deaths each year in large cities.
  • Deforestation — Deforestation of the Amazon has reached alarming levels in recent years.
  • Recycling — Recycling is only part of the solution; reducing consumption is equally important.
  • Climate change — Climate change is no longer a future prediction, but a reality we are living.
  • Waste — Urban waste management is a growing challenge for local administrations.
  • Ecology — Each ecosystem has a fragile balance that can be disrupted by human interventions.

Economy and Business

Economic vocabulary is essential for understanding journalistic texts and participating in discussions about current events in the oral exam.

  • Entrepreneurship — Youth entrepreneurship has grown thanks to new technologies and access to funding.
  • Inflation — Inflation has reduced the purchasing power of middle-class families.
  • Investment — Foreign investment is an indicator of confidence in a country's economy.
  • Unemployment — The youth unemployment rate exceeds 30% in some European countries.
  • Competitiveness — The competitiveness of companies increasingly depends on technological innovation.
  • Globalization — Globalization has transformed production and distribution chains worldwide.
  • Budget — The state budget allocates an increasing percentage to health and education.
  • Productivity — Increasing productivity without sacrificing quality of life is the great labor challenge.
  • Taxation — Progressive taxation seeks to ensure that those who have more contribute proportionally more.
  • Foreign trade — Foreign trade represents more than 60% of GDP in open economies.

Culture and Art

Culture and art are common topics in written expression tests (reviews) and oral expression (presentations about cultural events).

  • Heritage — Intangible cultural heritage includes traditions, languages, and artistic expressions.
  • Avant-garde — The artistic avant-gardes of the 20th century broke all established conventions.
  • Patronage — Private patronage complements public funding for museums and galleries.
  • Cultural identity — Cultural identity is built through language, customs, and collective memory.
  • Diversity — Cultural diversity enriches societies and fosters creativity.
  • Censorship — Artistic censorship still exists in many countries in different forms.
  • Cultural industry — The cultural industry generates millions of jobs and contributes significantly to GDP.
  • Masterpiece — Picasso's Guernica is considered a masterpiece of contemporary art.
  • Trend — Current artistic trends reflect the social concerns of our time.
  • Exhibition — The artist's retrospective exhibition attracted more than 200,000 visitors.

Science and Technology

Scientific and technological advances are frequent topics in the reading comprehension texts of DELE B2.

  • Artificial intelligence — Artificial intelligence is transforming sectors such as medicine, education, and transportation.
  • Research — Scientific research requires stable funding and academic freedom.
  • Innovation — Technological innovation is advancing faster than the ability of laws to regulate it.
  • Digital divide — The digital divide between generations and between countries continues to widen.
  • Cybersecurity — Cybersecurity has become a priority for companies and governments.
  • Algorithm — Social media algorithms determine what information we consume daily.
  • Biotechnology — Biotechnology offers promising solutions for previously incurable diseases.
  • Big data — Big data analysis allows for predicting consumer trends with great precision.
  • Technological ethics — Technological ethics raises questions about the limits of automation.
  • Sustainable development — Sustainable development seeks to reconcile technological progress with environmental protection.

Interpersonal Relationships

This lexical field is essential for oral expression tests, where topics about coexistence and human relationships are often discussed.

  • Empathy — Empathy is the ability to put yourself in someone else's shoes and understand their emotions.
  • Coexistence — Coexistence in multicultural societies requires mutual respect and dialogue.
  • Commitment — Commitment in a relationship involves constant effort from both parties.
  • Conflict — Resolving a conflict constructively strengthens long-term relationships.
  • Bond — Family bonds remain fundamental in Hispanic culture.
  • Tolerance — Tolerance does not mean accepting everything, but respecting legitimate differences.
  • Prejudice — Prejudices are fought with education, intercultural contact, and critical thinking.
  • Solidarity — Neighborhood solidarity was key during the most difficult moments of the pandemic.
  • Self-esteem — Healthy self-esteem is the foundation for establishing balanced relationships with others.
  • Reconciliation — Reconciliation between work and personal life remains a challenge for many families.

False Friends at Level B2

False friends are words that look similar in Spanish and other languages but have different meanings. At level B2, these mistakes can cost you important points:

  • Actual — Does not mean "actual" (in English), but "present, of now". The current situation of the labor market is concerning.
  • Realizar — Does not always equate to "realize" (to become aware). In Spanish, it means "to carry out". They conducted a study on consumption habits.
  • Sensible — Does not mean "sensible" (in English, wise), but "sensitive". She is a very sensitive person who gets emotional easily.
  • Éxito — Does not mean "exit" (exit), but "positive result, triumph". The success of the campaign exceeded all expectations.
  • Pretender — Does not mean "pretend" (to fake), but "to try, to aspire to". I intend to finish the master's degree before June.
  • Soportar — Does not mean "support" (to help), but "to endure, to tolerate". I can't stand the constant noise from the construction.
  • Embarazada — Does not mean "embarrassed" (ashamed), but "pregnant". My sister is five months pregnant.
  • Constipado — Does not mean "constipated" (having constipation), but "having a cold". I have had a cold for three days due to the temperature change.
  • Carpeta — Does not mean "carpet" (rug), but "folder" or "portfolio". Store all the documents in that blue folder.
  • Compromiso — Does not always equate to "compromise" (mutual agreement). In Spanish, it can mean "obligation" or "engagement". I have an unavoidable commitment this afternoon.

B2 Level Connectors and Discourse Markers

In addition to thematic vocabulary, DELE B2 requires the correct use of advanced connectors that provide cohesion to your texts and speeches:

  • However — formal equivalent of "but". The results were positive. However, there is still much to improve.
  • Regarding — to introduce a specific topic. Regarding education, more resources are needed.
  • Although — concession with indicative or subjunctive. Although it is raining, we will go for a walk.
  • Given that — formal cause. Given that there is no agreement, the vote will be postponed.
  • Hence — consequence (+ subjunctive). Demand has increased; hence, prices have risen.
  • Ultimately — to conclude. Ultimately, the reform will benefit most citizens.
  • Consequently — formal consequence. Deadlines were not met; consequently, penalties will be applied.
  • As long as — condition (+ subjunctive). You will pass as long as you study consistently.

Memorization Strategies for B2 Vocabulary

Memorizing advanced vocabulary requires more sophisticated techniques than simple repetition. Here are the most effective strategies:

1. Learn in context, never isolated words

Instead of memorizing lists, read articles from Spanish newspapers (El País, La Vanguardia) and underline new words. Write down the complete sentence where they appear, not just the word.

2. Use spaced repetition cards

Applications like Anki or the vocabulary function of DELE B2 Practice use spaced repetition algorithms that optimize long-term memorization. Review the words just before you forget them.

3. Create thematic mind maps

Group vocabulary by semantic fields (as in this article) and create visual connections between related words. The brain retains information better when organized in networks.

4. Practice active production

It is not enough to recognize the words: you need to use them. Write short texts (100-150 words) using 5-6 new words each day. This transforms passive vocabulary into active.

5. Associate words with personal situations

Relate each new word to a personal experience or a vivid mental image. Emotional connections reinforce memory.

6. Listen and repeat aloud

Correct pronunciation helps fix words in memory. Listen to podcasts in Spanish and repeat new expressions paying attention to intonation.

Vocabulary Study Plan for DELE B2

To cover all the necessary vocabulary, we recommend this 8-week plan:

  • Weeks 1-2: Politics and society + Causal and consequential connectors
  • Weeks 3-4: Environment + Economy + Concessive connectors
  • Weeks 5-6: Science and technology + Culture + Organizational connectors
  • Weeks 7-8: Interpersonal relationships + General review + False friends

Dedicate 20-30 minutes daily to vocabulary: 10 minutes of reading with notes, 10 minutes of review with cards, and 10 minutes of written or oral production.

Conclusion

The vocabulary of DELE B2 reflects a speaker's ability to navigate formal contexts and discuss complex topics. It is not just about knowing many words, but knowing how to use them accurately and in the appropriate register. Combine systematic study with natural exposure to the language (press, podcasts, series) and you will see how your lexicon expands organically.

Remember that the exam does not assess isolated words, but your ability to understand and produce coherent texts on varied topics. Always practice in context and prioritize quality over quantity.
