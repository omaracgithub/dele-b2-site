Reading Comprehension DELE B2: Task Strategies

The reading comprehension test of the DELE B2 lasts 70 minutes and consists of 4 tasks. It is the first test of the exam and is part of Group 1 along with written expression. Here we explain each task with specific strategies to maximize your score.

Overview of the Test

You have 70 minutes to complete 4 tasks with a total of 36 items. This means approximately 17 minutes per task. The key is not to get stuck on a question: mark your best option and move on.

Task 1: Long Text with Multiple Choice Questions

What do they ask you?

Read an informative or argumentative text (600-700 words) and answer 6 multiple choice questions (a, b, c).

Strategies

  • Read the questions first (not the options) to know what to look for in the text
  • The questions follow the order of the text: the answer to question 1 is before that of question 2
  • Look for paraphrases: the correct answer never uses the same words as the text, but rather synonyms or reformulations
  • Eliminate trap options: they often use words from the text but with a different meaning

Task 2: Match Texts with Statements

What do they ask you?

Read 4 short texts (from different people expressing opinions on a topic) and match 10 statements with the corresponding text. Some texts may be associated with more than one statement.

Strategies

  • Read the 4 texts first and note the main idea of each
  • Identify each person's stance: in favor, against, neutral, with nuances?
  • Look for meaning matches, not exact words
  • If you doubt between two texts, reread the statement looking for the nuance that differentiates them

Task 3: Place Fragments in a Text

What do they ask you?

Read a text from which 6 fragments have been extracted and place them in their correct position. There is one extra fragment that acts as a distractor.

Strategies

  • Pay attention to grammatical clues: pronouns, connectors, and agreements indicate which fragment fits
  • Follow the thread of meaning: each fragment continues the previous idea and introduces the next one
  • Start with the gaps you are sure about and leave the doubtful ones for last
  • Reread the complete text with your answers to check that it flows coherently

Task 4: Gaps with Multiple Choice

What do they ask you?

Complete a text with 14 gaps choosing between three options (a, b, c) for each. Your command of vocabulary, grammar, and connectors is evaluated.

Strategies

  • Read the complete sentence before choosing: the context defines the correct option
  • Pay attention to grammar: prepositions, verb tenses, subjunctive, and agreement
  • Watch for collocations: many options depend on which word usually accompanies another
  • Do not leave gaps blank: there is no penalty for errors, so always mark your best option

Time Management

Allocate the 70 minutes according to the difficulty of each task, not equally. Tasks 1 and 2 usually require more reading; tasks 3 and 4 rely more on specific clues. Reserve the last 5 minutes to review and transfer your answers.
